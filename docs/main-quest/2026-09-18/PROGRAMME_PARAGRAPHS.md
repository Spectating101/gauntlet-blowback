# Programme-specific research statements

## NYCU — intelligent systems

I would bring to an intelligent-systems doctorate a question that has become central to my software work: how can an AI system study the approaches to a problem before proposing another solution? Refinery is where I want to develop this. The first study would examine how systems for reconciling research data differ in their methods and assumptions, and whether AI-generated comparisons help a reader understand those differences. My finance research gives me a practical setting in which to begin. I want doctoral training to deepen the information-retrieval and software-analysis methods behind the work, then test how far they transfer to other problems.

## NTU — GINM

GINM's work in information retrieval and knowledge discovery is relevant to the part of Refinery I want to study most closely. A repository search can return projects that appear relevant, but the next step is understanding which parts of a problem they actually address. I would investigate representations that describe a project's approach from its source material and support comparisons across projects, even when they use different terminology. I would start with research-data tools and evaluate the comparisons against independently reviewed source material. That would give the work a clear retrieval question and an empirical test, while leaving room to examine other domains later.

## NCCU — MIS academic track

I would frame the project around how researchers learn about the available solutions to a problem. Refinery would assemble and compare descriptions of software approaches, with enough source detail for a reader to check the comparison. I would first test the quality of those descriptions, then examine whether they change how people investigate a problem or decide what to build. My background combines finance research with software development, so I am interested in both the implementation and its effect on the work of an analyst. I would want the evaluation to distinguish a useful change in that work from a system that simply produces more polished summaries.
