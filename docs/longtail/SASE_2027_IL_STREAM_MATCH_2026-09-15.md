## Why Network J is the default

SASE describes Network J as the network for transformations of economic life produced by digital technologies. Its stated interests include:

- digital market processes and actors;
- platform-economy intermediaries and business models;
- large-scale consumer-data collection and new forms of calculation;
- digital-market governance and regulation;
- governmental digital initiatives and services;
- governance capacity and wider socio-economic outcomes;
- plural empirical methods, including statistical and digital-trace analysis.

That is a direct fit for IL's actual research contribution: different platform, corporate, statistical, payment and administrative records observe different units and therefore construct materially different views of the same digital economy.

The SASE framing should therefore be about **the institutional production of economic visibility**, not the superseded claim that a single residual measures a hidden economy.

## Recommended standing-network contribution frame

Working proposition after the sample perimeter is ratified:

> Digital markets do not generate one administratively neutral economic record. Platform transaction measures, issuer accounting, official business statistics, payment traces and tax-administration records observe different units, apply different recognition rules and expose different parts of the same commercial system. Invisible Ledger studies how those ledgers diverge, where they can be reconciled, and what those differences imply for market measurement and state visibility.

Empirical anchor after ratification:

- Indonesia remains the proposed main geography;
- strict Indonesia claims remain tied to Indonesia-specific/statistical/payment/institutional evidence;
- direct issuer longitudinal translation may use scope-labelled Tokopedia, Blibli 3P and Bukalapak Group histories only if the advisor approves that inference architecture;
