## Grants, gigs and practical capacity

The [funding map](PHD_FUNDING_MAP_2026-10-28.md) records individual leads and eligibility; it is dated and must be refreshed before action. Supervisor-funded projects can pay a separately contracted RA, but a project budget is not all personal income. A small external grant can add funds only if awarded, its budget allows personal compensation and it is compatible with the university, visa and other awards. Equipment, compute credits and reimbursements are cost offsets, not wages. Industry fellowships often need university nomination, later PhD standing or restrict overlapping awards. Do not count a fellowship twice as both a grant and RA salary. Do not use speculative gig or grant amounts for rent commitments.

The user's current RA/TA experience is real evidence that combining work and study can be feasible, but the actual PhD terms, research demands and hours must be checked. 1. Request/read each award letter: monthly amount, exact source split, guaranteed months, renewal, tuition waiver and first payment date. Check any transition period and fifth year.
2. Identify the adviser and ask whether paid RA/TA work is genuinely additional to matching, with duties, monthly amount, hours, duration and whether outside work and grants are allowed. Do not presume that professors have committed.

4. For each job contender recheck the actual post and confirm written base, guaranteed months, variable pay, duties, work arrangement and start date. A fair comparison is actual spendable cash and research opportunity under both arrangements, not the most optimistic end of both.
5. Pursue the PhD when the supervisor and dependable package make it attractive; choose a strong aligned job if its actual offer and work beat the doctorate available. Keep both tracks open until evidence exists. No application, acceptance or outreach is authorized by this research record.

## Additional scale: how much work is required for NT$70K?

Assuming a full 12 months of base support and no other income, the additional monthly gross required is NT$50K, 40K or 30K for base awards of 20K, 30K or 40K respectively. These are mathematical targets, not expected earnings. A Type B tuition-only award would need the whole 70K from elsewhere. To turn additional work into an annual average, count real funded months: an additional 20K paid for only nine months contributes 15K/month averaged over the year.

| Total scholarship, including required matching | Extra needed for 70K | Example 70K combination | Average additional hourly rate if 20 hours/week are available |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| 20K | 50K | 20K base + 20K additional RA/TA + 30K gig | ~577/hour |
| 30K | 40K | 30K base + 20K additional RA/TA + 20K gig | ~462/hour |
| 40K | 30K | 40K base + 20K additional RA/TA + 10K gig | ~346/hour |

The hourly figures divide the required extra income by 20 × 52/12 = 86.7 paid hours per month. They are arithmetic sensitivity tests for fully billable qualifying work, not an assumption of permission or available clients. At a hypothetical 300/hour, a 30K increment requires 100 hours/month, about 23.1 hours/week; at 500/hour it needs 60 hours/month, about 13.8 hours/week. Finding such rates and keeping hours billable are unproven. The weekly rule is a weekly cap, not a monthly bank of hours; 86.7 is an annual-average conversion only. If required matching duties are classified as work and already take 8 hours/week, just 12 hours/week remain under a 20-hour aggregate limit for other work, making a 30K increment require roughly 577/hour. Confirm actual classification with the university and relevant authority; unpaid research, coursework, administration, client acquisition and other demands still take time even if not counted under that limit. [NYCU's international student guidance](https://oia.nycu.edu.tw/oia/en/app/artwebsite/view?id=812&module=artwebsite&serno=5a3dac2c-c240-4576-a243-46b1bfcba3f6) states that foreign students' combined working hours generally may not exceed 20 per week outside winter and summer vacations.

## Equivalent job salary under different costs

Before taxes and excluded costs, a job needs gross monthly pay equal to **PhD inflow + job monthly costs − PhD monthly costs** for the same remaining cash. The costs here include housing, routine living and transport. They are hypothetical scenarios, not actual quotes or an assertion that jobs always cost more.

| PhD gross monthly inflow | PhD costs 16K; job costs 24K | Both cost 20K | PhD costs 24K; job costs 20K |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 50K | 58K job | 50K job | 46K job |
| 60K | 68K job | 60K job | 56K job |
| 70K | 78K job | 70K job | 66K job |

Differences in tax treatment, health insurance, tuition, annual bonuses, work hours, future raises and payment timing can materially change these numbers. A waiver has value only for fees actually avoided. Revisit the table using actual award letters, a realistic estimate of paid months, and a written job offer rather than comparing advertised maximums.
