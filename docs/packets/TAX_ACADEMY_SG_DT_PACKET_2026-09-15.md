## 4. One-sentence proposition

This project tests how Singapore converts commercially visible digital transactions into administrable GST obligations by assigning bounded duties to overseas suppliers, electronic marketplaces, domestic business buyers, and other transaction nodes under specific legal activation conditions.

## 5. Proposal summary — long form

Digital-tax debates often compare rates, nexus rules, or revenue outcomes, but those variables become administratively meaningful only after a tax system identifies a covered transaction, determines why it belongs to the jurisdiction, assigns a duty to a particular actor, and connects that duty to a repeatable collection or reporting process.

My current research develops a source-controlled transaction-path framework from seven digital-tax paths across Malaysia, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, and the Philippines. It defines a **fiscal choke point** as a legally activated transaction node at which a state assigns a bounded fiscal function to an actor positioned to observe or control part of the commercial event. The framework separates the taxable object, liable node, destination or nexus evidence, transaction rail, and reconciliation power, and then tests where platform or intermediary presence is insufficient to activate liability.

The proposed Tax Academy project extends that completed framework to **Singapore as an out-of-sample case**. Singapore is particularly useful because its GST architecture visibly separates several possible fiscal nodes. Under the Overseas Vendor Registration regime, an overseas supplier may be required to register, charge, and account for GST on qualifying B2C remote services and low-value goods; under specified conditions an electronic marketplace operator may instead be regarded as the supplier; GST-registered business buyers can face reverse-charge obligations for imported supplies; and the low-value-goods regime can allocate duties to additional intermediating actors such as qualifying redeliverers.

The study will code these as distinct transaction paths rather than treating Singapore as one country-level observation. It will test whether the existing propositions on **activation specificity** and **transaction-control constraints** survive the Singapore extension, and will separately map downstream filing, correction, refund, audit, dispute, and enforcement architecture where current official evidence permits.

The expected outputs are a Singapore transaction-path extension to the controlled dataset, a comparative research paper, a concise practitioner/policy brief, and seminar-ready presentation materials. Any practitioner validation will be used to test whether the legal architecture is operationally intelligible and correctly specified; it will not be used to claim causal effectiveness without suitable outcome data.

## 6. Short abstract — ~200 words

This project examines how Singapore converts digital commercial activity into administrable GST obligations. It extends a completed comparative framework covering seven transaction paths across Malaysia, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, and the Philippines. The framework defines a fiscal choke point as a legally activated transaction node at which a state assigns a bounded fiscal duty to an actor positioned to observe or control part of the commercial event.

Singapore provides a strong out-of-sample test because its Overseas Vendor Registration regime separates several possible fiscal nodes. Depending on the transaction and legal conditions, responsibility may attach to an overseas supplier, an electronic marketplace treated as the supplier, a GST-registered business buyer through reverse charge, or another qualifying intermediary in the low-value-goods chain. The project will code these paths separately through the taxable object, liable node, destination/nexus evidence, activation mechanism, transaction rail, filing/remittance cadence, and reconciliation power.

The research asks whether the existing propositions on activation specificity and transaction control survive the Singapore extension, while keeping legal assignment distinct from operational performance. Outputs will include a source-controlled Singapore extension, comparative paper, policy/practitioner brief, and seminar-ready presentation.

## 7. Research questions

1. **Placement** — Which actor carries each bounded GST function in Singapore’s cross-border digital-economy transaction paths?
2. **Activation** — Which conditions activate that responsibility: turnover/sales thresholds, customer belonging evidence, GST-registration status, deemed-supplier conditions, transaction type, payment/process control, or other statutory tests?
3. **Rail** — How tightly is the fiscal duty coupled to the transaction event, and how is it subsequently registered, filed, reported, corrected, refunded, audited, disputed, and enforced?
4. **Comparative test** — Does Singapore support, falsify, or refine the existing propositions that intermediary liability is activation-specific and that transaction-level fiscal action depends on access to relevant transaction/payment/process information?
5. **Administrative design** — Which parts of Singapore’s architecture reduce administrative distance, and where do compliance, correction, data, privacy, or intermediary-governance costs remain relevant?

## 8. Singapore extension design

### Candidate path S1 — Overseas supplier / OVR B2C

Current official IRAS material states that overseas businesses exceeding the relevant global-turnover and Singapore B2C supply thresholds must register under the OVR regime and account for GST on qualifying remote services and/or low-value goods.

Code:

- object: qualifying B2C remote service / LVG;
- node: overseas supplier;
- activation: turnover + Singapore B2C supply thresholds + covered supply;
- nexus evidence: customer belonging in Singapore, including approved evidence/proxies;
- rail: simplified pay-only registration and electronic filing;
- Stage-B questions: correction/refund/error handling, audit/review, penalties and documented compliance procedures.

### Candidate path S2 — Electronic marketplace operator as deemed supplier

IRAS states that under specified conditions a local or overseas electronic marketplace operator may be regarded as the supplier of covered B2C remote services or low-value goods made through the marketplace, making the operator responsible for charging and accounting for GST instead of the underlying supplier.

This is the strongest direct test of the existing fiscal-choke-point argument because marketplace status alone is not the analytical variable; the project must identify the **conditions that cause the marketplace to be treated as the supplier**.

### Candidate path S3 — B2B reverse charge

Singapore separately uses reverse charge for qualifying B2B imports where the GST-registered business is not entitled to full input tax recovery. This creates an alternative-node path in which fiscal action can move toward the domestic business buyer rather than an overseas vendor or marketplace.

### Candidate path S4 — Low-value-goods redeliverer / additional intermediary

IRAS material states that under specified conditions a qualifying redeliverer may be regarded as the supplier of low-value goods. Admit this as a separate path only after the governing source and conditions are fully coded. Do not collapse it into the marketplace path.

### Phase C — proposition stress test

Re-run the comparative propositions after admitting Singapore. The desired result is **not** confirmation at all costs. Record whether Singapore:

- supports an existing proposition;
- narrows it;
- supplies a countercase;
- or requires a new boundary condition.

### Phase D — practitioner validation

If permitted by Tax Academy and applicable institutional/ethics rules, use structured practitioner validation to check whether the coded legal/administrative path corresponds to how the OVR/marketplace regime is understood in practice. Validation targets classification and operational interpretation; it is not a substitute for causal outcome data.

If interviews or structured human-subject activity are not appropriate, substitute documented practitioner materials and seminar/workshop feedback without changing the core source-controlled design.

## 10. Why Singapore adds real information

Singapore is not being added to increase country count. It adds discriminating architecture:

- OVR supplier registration and simplified filing;
- explicit B2C/B2B separation;
- marketplace deemed-supplier rules;
- thresholds and customer-belonging evidence;
- remote-services and low-value-goods coverage;
- alternative placement through reverse charge;
- potentially separate redeliverer treatment.

This makes Singapore useful for testing whether the transaction-path framework travels to a highly formalized GST administration while preserving distinctions between supplier, marketplace, buyer and logistics/intermediary nodes.

## 11. Expected contribution to Tax Academy

The project is designed to produce something usable beyond a country survey:

- a precise map of **where Singapore places fiscal responsibility** in cross-border digital transactions;
- a comparative vocabulary for distinguishing supplier, marketplace, buyer and other intermediary duties;
- boundary conditions showing when digital visibility or marketplace participation does **not** by itself create liability;
- a structured separation between legal assignment and downstream operational capacity;
- an ASEAN/Southeast Asian comparison that can support practitioner discussion without pretending unlike tax instruments are directly equivalent.
