## 4. Frozen IPSA abstract — 316 words

How does institutional design determine which private actors become implementation nodes in digital-tax policy, and what administrative capacities are required for those arrangements to function? This paper answers that question through a transaction-path comparison of seven digital-tax arrangements in Malaysia, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, and the Philippines.

The analysis treats the legally distinct transaction path, rather than the country, as the unit of comparison. Using current legislation, regulations, tax-administration guidance, and official procedural material, each path is coded through the taxable object, liable node, destination or nexus evidence, activation mechanism, transaction rail, filing or remittance cadence, and reconciliation power. A second layer distinguishes node locus—where implementation responsibility is placed—from event coupling—how closely the assigned fiscal function is connected to payment or transaction-confirmation events. Boundary cases within the same national regime are used to test whether the apparent implementing actor actually carries the duty under changed transaction conditions.

Two comparative propositions emerge. First, intermediary liability is activation-specific: platform participation alone does not constitute an implementation arrangement. Thailand conditions the selected platform VAT role on control of a continuous process from offering through payment and delivery; Vietnam’s selected withholding path turns on online-ordering and payment functionality; Indonesia’s PMSE collector role requires formal appointment; and a Philippine countercase shows that marketplace facilitation without control of payment does not activate the cited marketplace VAT liability. Second, transaction-level fiscal action is most tightly coupled where the assigned node has contemporaneous access to payment or transaction-confirmation information.

The paper contributes to comparative policy implementation by conceptualizing fiscal-node placement as an institutional-design choice that distributes authority and administrative burden across public and private actors. It also separates formal implementation architecture from operational capacity: filing, correction, refund, audit, appeal, enforcement, and observed outcomes are coded as a distinct layer rather than inferred from the existence of a legal duty. This allows comparison of implementation arrangements without converting documented institutional design into unsupported claims about performance.

## 6. Contribution boundary

The IPSA version is not a renamed tax-conference abstract. Its contribution claim is narrower and more theoretical:

> A fiscal-node choice is an institutional implementation arrangement: it allocates bounded public functions to specific public/private actors, and its viability depends on the transaction information and downstream administrative capacities available to that configuration.

The paper can test/refine that proposition comparatively. It does **not** claim:

- that a particular arrangement maximises revenue or compliance;
- that platform-centred implementation is universally superior;
- that documented procedures demonstrate actual performance;
- that the cases identify causal policy effects;
- that all five countries instantiate the same tax instrument.
